Chronology Lab · Report I · Stages S1–S3 (mini) · 2026-07-26

Does thermodynamics forbid time travel to the past?

The claim ledger executed leaf by leaf, C1a → C7: every citation verified against primary sources, six miniature computational models run, verdicts assembled — for Kevin's review.

Evidence levels: E1 theorem · E2 explicit computation · E3 controlled heuristic · E4 verified literature · E5 intuition  ·  Provenance: every statement tagged verified / derived / computed / reported. Full ledger: docs/CLAIMS.md.

Verdict on the claim as stated: it does not survive — but it fails in an unusually honorable way. Two of its supporting legs are refuted (there is no global "energy of the universe" to conserve, and the "driven to 0" branch stabilizes time travel instead of forbidding it), and in every model system studied, self-consistent histories turn out to be generic rather than measure-zero-rare.

What survives is substantial. Its amplification mechanism is Hawking's chronology protection conjecture — the field's leading (still unresolved) argument against time machines. Its exact-match rule is the Novikov self-consistency principle, enforced automatically rather than by luck — and our winding-sum computation makes the claim's own cancellation mechanism concentrate all amplitude on exact energy match, just as Kevin proposed. And its "almost all" becomes a real number: a conspiratorial single gram costs probability ~10−2.2×10²² per pass — astronomically suppressed, never exactly zero.

Nearest true statements: T1 — macroscopic time machines plausibly self-destruct at formation (open conjecture). T2 — if loops exist, you can visit the past but never change it (well supported in studied models). T3 — macroscopic pastward intervention is entropically suppressed like exp(−ΔS/kB) (defensible, quantified here at toy level).

Each leaf below carries: the claim, a verdict chip, the evidence level the verdict rests on (E1 = proved within a stated framework … E5 = intuition), and what we actually found — with verbatim quotes from primary sources where they carry weight. Verdict leans were pre-registered before any evidence was gathered, so movement is visible; every movement is logged in the ledger's changelog. The claim under test (canonical statement)

"Almost all time travel to the past is impossible, because of the exponential growth potential of energy and disorder mismatches. If time travel is possible, then the universe's state — its enthalpy/entropy as well as its total energy — must be exactly the same when time returns to the present (the moment the jump to the past occurred). More specifically, all possible paths that do not result in an exactly equal energy state will be cancelled out. This is because of the infinite looping potential of jumps to the past: even a tiny difference will be driven toward infinity or zero as unlimited time-travel jumps occur." — Kevin, 2026-07-26 (canonical wording adopted at the claimant's direction the same day, replacing the informal capture — spelling and grammar repaired, substance unchanged: every tested term, including "enthalpy" and "total energy," is retained, so no verdict is affected)

Steelman. (1) Backward travel creates loops: present → past → present. (2) An acceptable history must return the universe's books — energy, entropy — exactly. (3) Loops compound without limit and amplify any mismatch toward ∞ or 0, eliminating ("cancelling") mismatched histories. (4) Exact matches are a measure-zero fine-tuning, so almost all attempted trips correspond to no allowed history.

One repair made before testing (logged): enthalpy was dropped — H = U + PV needs a constant-pressure environment, which the universe does not have; entropy carries that leg. And "the state when time returns" must be pinned to a model of travel: M1 one self-consistent history (there is no "original 2026 without the traveler" to compare against), M2 branching arrival (the constraint dissolves), or M3 a mutable timeline (requires a second time dimension physics doesn't have). The claim's dynamics run in M3; its conclusion belongs to M1; the best formulation of its cancellation — added later the same day at Kevin's suggestion — is stationary-phase interference (C6b), which needs no M3 at all.

Background, verified: general relativity really does admit time-travel solutions — Gödel's rotating universe (Rev. Mod. Phys. 21, 447 (1949): "it is theoretically possible in these worlds to travel into the past"), Gott's passing cosmic strings (PRL 66, 1126 (1991)), and the Morris–Thorne–Yurtsever wormhole time machine (PRL 61, 1446 (1988)), which needs energy-condition-violating exotic matter — itself squeezed hard by the Ford–Roman quantum inequalities (PRD 53, 5496 (1996): macroscopic traversable wormholes "very improbable"). Nothing below assumes machines exist; the question is what would constrain them if they did.

Leg C1 — energy: "total energy must return exactly"

C1a "The total energy of the universe is a well-defined, conserved quantity."
refutedE4 · verified literature

General relativity — the only framework in which pastward time travel is even formulable — does not define a conserved total energy for a generic universe. Conservation there is local (∇·T = 0, always enforced by the field equations); a global energy needs special structure the real universe lacks: a time-translation symmetry (Noether's theorem — no symmetry, no conservation law) or special asymptotics (ADM/Bondi mass). The expanding universe fails the claim's bookkeeping today, with no time machine involved: photons redshift and their energy simply is not accounted for globally; dark-energy density holds constant while volume grows.

"When the space through which particles move is changing, the total energy of those particles is not conserved." — Sean Carroll, "Energy Is Not Conserved" (2010) · preposterousuniverse.com · verified
"A local (point-like) density of energy associated with the gravitational field cannot be defined in General Relativity." — Jaramillo & Gourgoulhon (2010), citing Wald, General Relativity (1984) · arXiv:1001.5429 · verified

The Weiss–Baez physics-FAQ entry "Is Energy Conserved in General Relativity?" confirms exactly the split this leaf turns on: the local (differential) conservation law survives, the global (integral) form does not — and of the CMB's redshifted energy, "others will say that the energy is simply lost" (math.ucr.edu · verified).

Consequence. The claim's energy constraint cannot even be stated globally. It retreats to C1b (local bookkeeping) — where it is satisfied, not violated.

C1b "Transporting mass/energy to the past violates energy conservation."
refutedE4 · verified literature

In the standard time-machine construction (a traversable wormhole with time-shifted mouths), local conservation holds automatically everywhere — the field equations do not permit anything else. The books balance physically: the mouth an object enters gains its mass-energy, the mouth it exits loses it. And in the single-history model M1 there is no "extra copy" to account for: the traveler present during 1926–2026 was always part of that history's stress-energy.

"As this mass passes through the wormhole, the entrance mouth will gain mass while the exit mouth will lose mass by the same amount." — Cramer, Forward, Morris, Visser, Benford & Landis, PRD 51, 3117 (1995); mechanism credited therein to Frolov & Novikov, PRD 42, 1057 (1990) · arXiv:astro-ph/9409051 · verified

Verification note: the ledger originally pointed at Visser's book for this; the checkable primary turned out to be the Visser-coauthored paper above. Logged as a citation improvement — the content stands unchanged.

C1c "Around a closed worldline, conserved and state quantities must return to their values."
holds — vacuouslyE1 · by definition

True — but with no force. A closed worldline revisits the same events, so "returning to the same value" is just "being what it is there." The non-trivial cost of that periodicity is dynamical, and it belongs to entropy (C2a). Crucially, this constrains only genuinely closed worldlines. A one-shot traveler's worldline is not closed — it runs birth → machine → past → death, threading the loop once — so no periodicity applies to them at all.

Leg C2 — entropy: "disorder must return exactly"

C2a "Dissipation forbids closed worldlines — up to exponentially suppressed fluctuations."
holds (as suppression)E3 · derived here

The strongest surviving leg of the original claim. Derivation: along a closed timelike worldline every state function, entropy included, is periodic (C1c). The local second law says entropy production is ≥ 0 along the worldline. Zero net production around the loop with non-negative production everywhere forces exactly zero production at every point — the system must be perfectly reversible for its entire circuit, or else the history is a thermodynamic fluctuation priced at ~exp(−ΔS/kB). The modern second law (fluctuation theorems) makes that price precise: suppressed, never impossible.

Corollary (derived here; Landauer): recording a memory is irreversible, so nothing can remember completing a closed loop — every "I've looped forever" story is self-contradictory for a macroscopic rememberer. And N circuits compound: P ~ exp(−N·ΔS/kB) → 0 geometrically. This is the claim's "driven to 0," rigorous — as a probability of dissipative many-loop histories, not as a cancellation of energy.

C2b "A one-shot trip must leave the universe's total entropy unchanged."
refuted as statedE2 · computed (TM3)

No framework we examined imposes this. In M1 there is one history and entropy can rise monotonically along all of it, loop region included. In the quantum models the bookkeeping is model-dependent — and we computed it: Deutsch's consistency condition drives the loop system toward maximum entropy (our grandfather circuit's unique attractor is the maximally mixed state — see C3b figure), while postselected CTCs can lower entropy (postselection is not an ordinary physical evolution). What a trip does impose is correlation: arrivals from the future are boundary conditions on the past epoch. Exact entropy equality is nowhere required.

C2c "Enthalpy must match."
retired — category error

Enthalpy presupposes a pressure reservoir the universe does not have; it is not a cosmological state function. Repair logged on day one; the entropy leg (C2a) carries what this clause was reaching for.

Leg C3 — exactness: "only exact matches are allowed"

C3a "A time-travel history is acceptable iff the loop returns the state exactly."
holds in M1 (±ħ)E1 definition · E2 tolerance

In the single-history model this is true by construction — it is what "solution of the equations on a loop-bearing spacetime" means (the Novikov principle). Two sharpenings. First, the claim is too weak: the full microstate must match, not merely energy and entropy — matching those two is necessary, nowhere near sufficient. Second, from the action formulation (C6b), "exact" acquires a precise tolerance: histories within ~ħ of stationary action still add coherently. For any macroscopic mismatch that tolerance is astronomically exceeded — the classical "exact match" rule emerges in the ħ → 0 limit rather than being postulated. Kevin's "exactly equal" is right, and quantum mechanics even supplies the error bar.

C3b "Consistent solutions exist for generic initial data."
holds — against the claimE4 verified · E2 computed

The single strongest fact against the original claim. Wherever consistency has been checked concretely, solutions exist — they are not a fine-tuned rarity.

"…the only solutions to the laws of physics that can occur locally in the real Universe are those which are globally self-consistent." — Friedman, Morris, Novikov, Echeverria, Klinkhammer, Thorne & Yurtsever, PRD 42, 1915 (1990), pp. 1916–17 — the Novikov principle stated; the scalar-field Cauchy problem found "benign" in the wormhole spacetimes studied · CaltechAUTHORS · verified
"A search is made for initial trajectories with zero multiplicity, and none are found." — Echeverria, Klinkhammer & Thorne, PRD 44, 1077 (1991) — the billiard-ball grandfather paradox: every initial condition searched had at least one self-consistent solution (a search result, not a theorem — the paper is explicit) · CaltechAUTHORS · verified
"…closed timelike lines place no retrospective constraint on the state of a quantum system." — Deutsch, PRD 44, 3197 (1991), p. 3203 — fixed-point existence proved for every circuit; his grandfather bit lands in "the maximally mixed state ½·1" · full text · verified

Computed here (TM2): in a bounded-deflection self-interaction model, a consistent solution exists for every coupling scanned (a continuous map of a bounded interval into itself must cross the diagonal — existence by topology, the same logic that guarantees Deutsch's fixed points). Computed here (TM3): the noisy Deutsch grandfather map contracts to its consistent state from every random start, at rate 0.85 per circuit.

C3c "The consistent solution is unique."
refutedE4 verified · E2 computed

Generically no — and this inverts the claim's picture of time travel's pathology. The problem found in the literature and in our toys is not too few consistent histories but too many: physics stops predicting which one occurs.

"…all dangerous initial trajectories in a wide class have infinite multiplicity and thereby make the Cauchy problem ill posed in an unexpected way: 'far too many solutions.'" — Echeverria, Klinkhammer & Thorne, PRD 44, 1077 (1991), abstract · CaltechAUTHORS · verified

Stronger than our pre-registered lean: we expected multiplicity; the source shows infinite multiplicity for all "dangerous" data in a wide class. Deutsch handles his model's non-uniqueness by fiat — a "maximum entropy rule" selecting the highest-entropy fixed point (PRD 44, 3197, pp. 3204–05 · verified).

Optics analogy (same stationary-phase mathematics): where several stationary paths exist, all are realized — a gravitational lens shows multiple images of one quasar. Multiple consistent histories are the time-loop version of multiple images.

Leg C4 — amplification: "mismatches are driven to ∞ or 0"

C4a "Something traverses the loop unboundedly many times."
splitE4 · verified literature

For travelers: no — a one-shot trip is one circuit. For radiation: yes — at the moment a time machine would first form (the chronology horizon), closed light paths exist that a wave can traverse unboundedly often, and vacuum fluctuations do exactly that. Kevin's "infinite looping potential" is physically real — for field modes at the horizon, which is precisely where the serious physics of C4b lives.

C4b "Per-circuit gain > 1 drives the pile-up to ∞ and destroys the machine."
open — the frontierE4 · verified literature

This is Hawking's chronology protection conjecture — the original claim's mechanism, rediscovered. A wave exits the past mouth before it entered the future mouth, flies back, re-enters: per circuit it gains (blueshift) and loses (defocusing). If the net gain exceeds 1, the summed energy density diverges at the horizon and its gravity plausibly destroys the machine at birth. Thirty-plus years of literature have sharpened but not settled it:

"…the vacuum-polarization divergence gets cut off by quantum gravity upon reaching the tiny size lP/D, and spacetime remains macroscopically smooth and classical and develops closed timelike curves without difficulty." — Kim & Thorne, PRD 43, 3929 (1991) — the divergence computed, then conjectured harmless · INSPIRE · verified
"It seems the back reaction would prevent closed timelike curves from appearing… The laws of physics do not allow the appearance of closed timelike curves." — Hawking, PRD 46, 603 (1992), abstract. §I, p. 603: "It seems there is a chronology protection agency, which prevents the appearance of closed timelike curves and so makes the universe safe for historians." (Exact wording — the common variant is a slight misquote.) P. 610 adds the "hordes of tourists from the future" remark · full text · verified
"…quantities such as the renormalized expectation value of φ² or of the stress-energy tensor are necessarily ill-defined or singular at any base point." — Kay, Radzikowski & Wald, Commun. Math. Phys. 183, 533 (1997) — rigorous theorems: semiclassical gravity itself breaks down at compactly generated chronology horizons · arXiv:gr-qc/9603012 · verified

Verified loopholes that keep the question open: Ori's machine forms around a compact vacuum core with surrounding matter satisfying the weak, dominant, and strong energy conditions — "Stability remains the main open question" (PRL 95, 021101 (2005) · verified); Krasnikov exhibits quantum states with finite stress-energy up to the horizon (PRD 54, 7322 (1996) · reported via Visser's review) and separately proves classical GR can never force a time machine to form (CQG 19, 4109 (2002) · verified). The field's own status line:

"So, is chronology protected? Despite a decade's work we do not know for certain… if chronology protection is the answer, we will have to wander deep into the guts of quantum gravity to know for certain." — Visser, "The quantum physics of chronology protection" (2002) · arXiv:gr-qc/0204022 · verified

Status: unresolved; the rigorous semiclassical result (KRW) shows the framework itself breaks down at the horizon, escalating the verdict to quantum gravity. If protection holds, the true conclusion is stronger than the claim: all macroscopic time machines fail, not almost all.

C4c "Per-circuit gain < 1 — mismatch 'driven to 0' — also makes time travel impossible."
refuted — the horn backfiresE1 theorem · E2 computed

A loop map that damps mismatches is a contraction, and the Banach fixed-point theorem then guarantees a unique consistent history that nearby candidates converge toward. Damping doesn't delete histories; it deletes mismatches — making consistency stable, self-enforcing, robust. The claim's own dichotomy contains a pro-time-travel branch, and the quantum case sits on it: consistency-map dynamics are non-expansive, which is why Deutsch fixed points exist (C3b, left panel).

C4d "Marginal gain ≈ 1."
openE2 · computed (structure only)

The knife-edge case: mismatches neither die nor explode (TM1's flat line). Physically this is where interference structure decides (see C6b); classifying which real geometries sit here is future work — logged, not resolved.

Leg C5 — measure: "almost all"

C5a "Almost all" requires declaring a measure over something.
repair adopted

"Almost all X are impossible" is mathematics only once you say which space X lives in and which measure weighs it (over initial conditions? geometries? intervention strategies?). Every C5 verdict below names its measure. Without this repair the phrase is rhetoric — with it, the claim splits into one false version (C5b) and one defensible one (C5c).

C5b "With respect to natural measures on initial data, consistent solutions are measure-zero."
refuted in studied modelsE4 verified · E2 computed

In every system where the count has been done, the opposite holds: consistent solutions for all initial data tried (measure one, not zero) — the billiard literature (C3b), the quantum fixed-point theorem (C3b), and our TM2 scan (minimum solution count across the entire coupling range: 1, never 0). What gets suppressed is not solutions existing but particular naive stories — "I knock my earlier self fully off course, full stop" — which get replaced by nearby consistent ones (glancing blows), typically several.

C5c "Histories with macroscopic pastward intervention are entropically suppressed — 'almost all,' in the fluctuation measure."
holds in spiritE3 · derived + scaled

The defensible descendant of the claim's conclusion. A consistent history containing a macroscopic, dissipative, remembering traveler must realize conspiratorial correlations — the paradox-averting coincidences of Lewis's classic analysis. Fine-tuned macrostates carry an entropy deficit ΔS, and fluctuation statistics price them at ~exp(−ΔS/kB), compounding per circuit (C2a).

"Time travel, I maintain, is possible. The paradoxes of time travel are oddities, not impossibilities… he somehow fails… For some commonplace reason. Perhaps some noise distracts him at the last moment, perhaps he misses despite all his target practice, perhaps his nerve fails…" — David Lewis, "The Paradoxes of Time Travel," Am. Phil. Quarterly 13, 145 (1976) · full text · verified (the "banana peel" image often attached to this argument is a later gloss, not Lewis's own)

Leg C6 — cancellation: "non-matching paths are cancelled out"

C6a Postselection version: mismatched branches are projected out (P-CTCs).
holds in that frameworkE2 computed · E4 verified

In postselected closed timelike curves the loop is quantum teleportation postselected on loop-output = loop-input: every branch that fails the match is removed and the survivors renormalized — Kevin's sentence is close to the model's definition. Our TM3 computation shows the flagship consequence exactly: the perfect grandfather's postselection weight is cos²(π/2) = 0 — the paradoxical branch's amplitude cancels identically, and near-paradoxes are smoothly suppressed (C3b figure, right panel). Known costs of the model: nonlinearity, and outsized computational power.

"When the probability amplitude for the transition between these two states is null, we postulate that the related event does not happen (so that the Novikov principle is enforced)." — Lloyd et al., PRD 84, 025007 (2011) — the renormalized P-CTC map 𝒩[ρ] = CρC†/Tr[CρC†] with C = TrCTC[U]; photonic grandfather test in PRL 106, 040403 (2011) · arXiv:1007.2615 · verified

Computational price tags, verified with a corrected attribution: Deutsch CTCs make classical and quantum computing both equal PSPACE (Aaronson & Watrous, Proc. R. Soc. A 465, 631 (2009): "both would have the (extremely large) power of the complexity class PSPACE"); the P-CTC = PP statement is Lloyd et al.'s, resting on Aaronson's 2005 PostBQP = PP — our ledger had pinned it on the wrong paper, now fixed and logged. A Deutsch-CTC simulation has run on photons (Ringbauer et al., Nat. Commun. 5, 4145 (2014) · verified — "experimentally simulate," not a real CTC).

C6b Stationary-phase version: mismatched paths destructively interfere (sum over histories).
holds as mechanismE2 computed · E4 verified

Kevin's second-day upgrade: import the principle behind "light takes all paths; only the stationary one survives." Every loop configuration contributes amplitude eiS/ħ; configurations that fail to match around the loop have rapidly spinning phases and cancel; consistent ones sit at stationary action and dominate. No meta-time needed — the cancellation happens once, timelessly, in the amplitude sum.

"…the only possible trajectories … minimizing the classical action are those which are globally self-consistent, and … the 'Principle of self-consistency' (originally introduced by Novikov) is thus a natural consequence of the 'Principle of minimal action.'" — Carlini, Frolov, Mensky, Novikov & Soleng, Int. J. Mod. Phys. D 4, 557 (1995) · arXiv:gr-qc/9506087 · verified — Kevin's instinct, published 31 years earlier; relativistic-particle follow-up: Carlini & Novikov (1996), gr-qc/9607063 · verified

The sum-over-histories treatments exist and carry a verified warning label: Politzer's path integrals on CTC backgrounds find non-unitarity in some models and unitarity in others (PRD 46, 4470 (1992) · verified); Friedman–Papastamatiou–Simon prove "the unitarity relations are not satisfied for interacting fields" (PRD 46, 4456 (1992) · verified); Hartle's generalized quantum mechanics for nonchronal spacetimes rebuilds the formalism so probabilities stay consistent while transition matrices "may be non-unitary" (PRD 49, 6543 (1994) · verified). The mechanism is real; the resulting quantum theory pays for it.
